## 2. What shipped

**44 PNGs and one SVG**, in `assets/packs/v0/`, generated by
`tools/gen_assets.gd` from the same `PacketStyle` constants the whitebox drew
from. That is what makes v0 *provably* reproduce the whitebox rather than
approximate it — and the proof turned out to be mechanical: after Phase E pruned
seven dead registry members, the pack regenerated **byte-identical**.

**Three types**, under `scenes/graphics/`:

- `GraphicsPack` — a `Resource` whose exported fields **are** the contract. A
  missing asset is a null field, so `validate()` walks it mechanically. It reads
  the property list rather than a maintained list, and array sizes come from the
  enums, so adding a Packet shape fails the pack instead of silently rendering
  MISSING.
- `PacketPalette` — the SVG parser, accepting both `fill=` and `style="fill:"`
  because Inkscape converts between them on save. Shared with `check_assets`, so
  the checker cannot certify a file the game can't read.
- `Graphics` — the accessor. Semantics, never paths; bounds-checked, because the
  renderer indexes it from game state and game state must never crash the view.

**Startup validation reports every problem at once and does not block.** Content
has no fallback and must stop launch; graphics has the MISSING checker by
design, and refusing to launch would turn a cosmetic fault into an unplayable
game. The checker is generated rather than shipped — a pack cannot be trusted to
contain the texture that reports the pack is broken.

## 4. What the verification caught, and what only eyes could

Three defects reached a device. **All three were invisible to the suite**, and
the pattern is worth stating: the catalog tests answer *is the asset present*.
Every one of these was a question about *how it got drawn*.

### 4.1 `draw_texture_rect` stretches; only `draw_style_box` nine-patches

The whole conversion rests on 9-slicing — chrome authored at 48×48 with a 16 px
margin so a 1 px border stays 1 px at any size. That works through `Theme`, and
not at all through `CanvasItem.draw_texture_rect`, which stretches the image into
the rect. Every authored border scaled with its control: a 2 px edge became
roughly **24 px** on a 590 px-wide Program box, and the Deck Function's "ready"
frame swallowed its own charge readout.

Worth keeping: **the failure scaled with the control**, so it was near-invisible
on the small boxes and blatant on the one wide one — the worst distribution,
because a glance says "fine" and the single bad control reads as a quirk of that
control.

### 4.2 and 4.3 — measurements that crossed from code into art (D-040)

Twice, a number the layout engine used to compute became a property of an image,
and both times the first attempt shipped the wrong value:

- The Build slot's accent bar was `maxi(2, px(4))` ≈ 10 px. Authored at 4 px it
  rendered 4 px, because a 9-slice preserves its corner region 1:1.
- The reorder arrows were text, and a Label overflows its container's padding
  happily. A texture does not — `expand_icon` shrank the mark to the ~15 px the
  button's padding left it.

**Both were caught by comparing against the pre-conversion captures**, not by
looking at the new screen and asking whether it seemed fine. That is the
argument for taking a before set at all, and it is the single most useful process
change in this build.

## 6. Two findings handed back

### AN-007 — the type ring diverged from the alpha, undocumented

The beta drew a coloured ring outside the ownership badge to carry an overlay's
type. **The alpha never had one** — `view.ts` carries type on the badge's centre
mark alone. It was never recorded, and it widened the overlay from the alpha's
0.45 × cell to 0.61, which is most of why a compact glyph vanished underneath
one.

The interesting part is not the ring. It survived beta 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3, four
device gates, and a full Boss battle played to completion, and **nothing in the
project could see it**. The differential compares behaviour; the tests do not
touch the scene layer; `test_presentation.gd` enforces where appearance
decisions live but not whether they are *right*. It was caught by a person
remembering the reference.

Suspended rather than deleted, pending the designer.

### AN-008 — the pause menu called every battle a "Quick Match"

**Fixed on instruction.** It was a literal, set once and never updated, and had
been wrong for every Run battle since 0.2. Two more of the same class were found
in the same file: battle messages calling a Boss "System", and the header's
placeholder title being the literal `SYSTEM` that P-042 left on screen for a
whole build.

**This is the P-043 shape for the third, fourth and fifth time** — a string
encoding a fact about the build, written where the fact was true, with nothing
downstream that would notice when it stopped being. The director has scheduled a
text pass to bring strings into a framework; that pass is the right place to
make this class structurally impossible rather than repeatedly fixed.

## 7. Also fixed: a test that was not running

Chasing an unexplained `SCRIPT ERROR` in an otherwise green suite found that
`test_boss.gd` read `Content.function(FN_CODESHATTER)["damage"]`. A Function row
has no `damage` — it carries `plan`. In GDScript a missing Dictionary key
**aborts the calling function**, so eight assertions after that line had never
executed, covering four numbered requirements, while the suite counted the rest
and reported `0 failed`.

All eight pass now that they run. The gameplay was correct; the claim that it
had been verified was not. **The Beta 0.3 handback's "3,122 tests passing"
should be read with this in mind** — honestly reported, and eight of the
assertions behind it were not doing their job.

**A passing suite that prints errors is not a passing suite.**

## 8. The S25 pass

**Signed off on hardware, 2026-08-27.** The emulated viewport had already
cleared the geometry; this window covered the two things it structurally could
not.

- **Safe area over the real cutout** ✅ — the battle header sits below the notch
  with a visible top inset, applied from `DisplayServer.get_display_safe_area()`
  at runtime rather than from any constant. Nothing is under the cutout.
- **Overlay-mark legibility on the panel** ✅ — and this was the one that
  mattered, because with the type ring suspended the mark carries the whole type
  signal. All four are crisp and mutually distinguishable at panel density, in
  both ownership polarities, alongside both armed countdown states.
- No horizontal clipping; all eight board columns and all six debug controls
  present with symmetric margins.
- **Charged vs ready are distinct on the panel** — MUSCLE at 7/7 wears the amber
  frame, AGIMA at 3/3 the white one. That is §16.2's requirement that interaction
  states not go ambiguous under asset scaling, and it is the check most at risk
  when a 2 px drawn border becomes a scaled texture.
- Clean log: engine banner and GPU line, nothing else.

**Worth recording:** the emulated pass was right about everything it could see,
and the two things it could not see were exactly the two that needed the phone.
That is the same division of labour 0.3 found, and it holds — reach for
`wm size` first, and spend the device window on the cutout and the panel.

## 9. For the next iteration

The authorization names the **graphics-development jig** as what follows, and
0.3.1 was shaped to make it cheap: the jig reuses `GraphicsPack`, `Graphics` and
the production components rather than growing a parallel art-only renderer. The
palette path was chosen partly for this — the jig reads the SVG through the same
`PacketPalette` the game uses.

Three things worth deciding before or during it:

1. **The overlay design** (AN-007) — the ring is suspended, not decided. The
   bomb mark's meaning also changed from "unknown" to "a bomb", which is a
   design call I made and flagged rather than buried.
2. **The text pass** — countdown digits, the string framework, and typography
   are all deferred here and interlock.
3. **The scene layer still has no instrument.** Three of this project's defects
   have now been found by a human comparing against a reference. The before/after
   capture pair was the cheapest thing that has ever worked for this; making it a
   routine artefact of each build, rather than something done once because the
   graphics pass invited it, is the obvious next step.
